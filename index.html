    E–k Bandgap Transitions

    Optical absorption is vertical in k (photons carry ~0 crystal momentum). Indirect gaps need a phonon to finish the jump in k-space — slower, and some energy dumps into lattice heat (slide 43).

    Select a gap type, then fire a photon from the valence-band maximum at k = 0.

    A photon can pay the energy bill. It cannot change the electron’s crystal momentum. Silicon has to borrow that Δk from a vibrating lattice.

    Energy bands · recombination

    An injected electron sits in the conduction band. Recombination can release that gap energy as a photon.

    The diagram is a map of electron energy, not a picture of an electron tumbling through the chip. Direct-gap materials (GaAs, InGaN) let the electron and hole share the same crystal momentum, so a photon can carry the energy by itself. Indirect-gap crystals (Si, and GaP to a large extent) also need a phonon — most of the energy ends up as heat.

    A blue LED is not “brighter voltage.” It is a larger gap, so each injected electron must be lifted farther in energy before it can recombine.

    Bluer is not a different kind of electron. It is a larger Eg, a higher-energy photon, a shorter wavelength — and, to first order, a larger VF.

    I–V meets the load line

    Why not a fixed R?

    Rstatic = Vd / I
    rd ≈ n VT / I

    Rstatic is the chord from the origin to Q. rd is the local slope — what a small signal actually sees. Neither is printed on the diode.

    The exponential is not a personality trait of the symbol. It is a shrinking barrier dumping a Boltzmann tail of carriers across a junction — and the resistor decides how far that can go.
